# Micro QR Code (`breader-qr`, milestone M11)

Micro QR (versions M1 to M4) is read by the QR reader (`QrReader`): the same finder scan serves both, so a frame pays for one scan, not two. A Micro QR result carries `Symbology::MicroQr`; it can be switched off with `ScanOptions::symbologies` independently of QR Code. Like QR it works on the binary plane in both polarities (`uses_binary() == true`, `handles_inversion() == true`) and also looks at the luma image for the retries. Part of the cross-cutting [architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md); status and spec references are in the [symbology index](README.md); the QR design it builds on is [qr.md](qr.md).

## Scope

In scope: M1 (11x11, numeric, error detection only), M2 (13x13, numeric and alphanumeric, L and M), M3 (15x15, plus byte and kanji, L and M), M4 (17x17, all four modes, L, M and Q), any rotation, perspective, light-on-dark and mirrored (transposed) symbols. Out of scope: ECI, FNC1 and Structured Append (Micro QR has none of them; unknown mode indicators are rejected), rMQR, QR Model 1. Normative source: ISO/IEC 18004:2015 (the Micro QR clauses). The standard text was not available while writing this; every constant was written from memory of the standard and checked against two independent things, an encoder (the `qrcode` crate as a dev-dependency, which supports `Version::Micro(1..=4)`) and the real symbols of the zxing-cpp samples (`microqrcode-1`, produced by other encoders). Where the two disagree the real symbols win (see [as built](#as-built)).

## Modules and data flow

Code lives in `crates/breader-qr/src/micro/`: `spec` (sizes, the eight symbol numbers with data and check codewords, data placement, mask patterns, character count widths), `decode` (format information, unmasking, Reed-Solomon, `DecodedMicro`), `bits` (segments and padding), `geom` (finder pose, timing lines, mapping), `refine` (corner refinement by sampling sharpness), `reader` (orchestration), `tests` and `reader_tests`.

One pass of `QrReader::scan`: the finder search (shared, both polarities) -> QR triplets as before -> for each polarity, the finders not inside a decoded QR or Micro QR quad become Micro QR candidates, strongest first, capped by effort.

## Stages

1. **Candidates.** The finders of the QR scan (ratio 1:1:3:1:1, both polarities) that lie inside no decoded symbol; a finder inside a decoded Micro QR quad is skipped like one inside a QR quad. Cap per pass: 4 (Fast), 10 (Balanced), 40 (Exhaustive). A candidate needs a module of at least 1.4 px.
2. **Finder outline.** The 9x9 finder template (7x7 pattern plus a light border) is fitted over rotation, module size and center (coarse angle scan in 6 degree steps, then a local search, ties averaged). For modules of 3 px or more the center and size are then set from the outer edges of the ring, because the template score is flat over a large part of a module. The four sides are measured by walking from the dark ring outwards to the first light pixel at six points per side; their line intersections give the four finder corners (perspective included), with the similarity corners as fallback.
3. **Orientation and size.** The timing patterns run along row 0 and column 0 from module 8 on, so the symbol lies in one of four quadrants relative to the finder (the pose turned by 0 to 3 quarters; mirroring is handled by decoding the transposed grid). For each quadrant a homography from the finder corners reads both timing lines in module coordinates: sampling at 1/8 module along row 0 (column 0), the transitions must be dark-to-light at module 7 (separator), then alternate at 8, 9, ... within 0.45 module of a progressively fitted position, end after 5, 7, 9 or 11 edges (M1 to M4), and be followed by at least one and a half modules without an edge. Both lines must give the same size. In the first round one clean line may propose the size, the fit must then confirm it on both. The edges and the outer boundary of the finder's top row and left column and of the dark timing modules (top and left boundary of the symbol) refit the homography (three rounds). The size is therefore determined by the timing patterns, not guessed.
4. **Sampling and gates (before Reed-Solomon).** The ring one module outside the symbol must be light in 75 % of the samples inside the image (quiet zone). The grid is sampled (majority of five sub-samples from 3 px per module). Required: finder and separator (64 modules) with at most 6 mismatches, timing patterns alternating on both sides with no error in M1 and at most (d - 8) / 3 in the others (1, 2, 3 for M2 to M4). Only then the decoder runs.
5. **Format information.** 15 bits at row 8 columns 1 to 8 and column 8 rows 1 to 7 (bit 0 at column 8, row 1; bits 7 to 14 along row 8 from column 8 down to 1), BCH(15,5) with the QR generator, XOR 0x4445. A single copy exists, so the accepted Hamming distance to the nearest valid word is at most 2 (QR accepts 3 with two copies). The symbol number (0 M1, 1 M2-L, 2 M2-M, 3 M3-L, 4 M3-M, 5 M4-L, 6 M4-M, 7 M4-Q) must match the sampled size, otherwise the grid is rejected. The two mask bits select one of the four Micro QR patterns (row even; (row/2 + column/3) even; ((rc) mod 2 + (rc) mod 3) even; ((r+c) mod 2 + (rc) mod 3) even).
6. **Placement and error correction.** Data modules in the two-column zigzag from the bottom right, skipping finder, separator, timing and format modules (no vertical timing column, so no column skip). One Reed-Solomon block over GF(256) (the QR field). The data codewords are 3 (M1), 5/4 (M2-L/M), 11/9 (M3-L/M), 16/14/10 (M4) with check codewords 2, 5/6, 6/8, 8/10/14; M1 and M3 end in a half codeword (20 and 84 or 68 bits): the check codewords start at the next module, and for the code the half codeword is its four bits followed by four zeros, which must still be zero after correction. Misdecode guard: errors corrected `<= (ec - reserve) / 2` with reserve 2 up to 8 check codewords and 1 above, so M1 corrects nothing (any nonzero syndrome rejects), M2-L 1, M2-M 2, M3-L 2, M3-M 3, M4-L 3, M4-M 4, M4-Q 6.
7. **Bitstream.** Mode indicator of 0, 1, 2 or 3 bits (M1 none, numeric only; M2 numeric/alphanumeric; M3 and M4 add byte and kanji), character count of 3 to 6 bits (numeric), 3 to 5 (alphanumeric), 4 to 5 (byte), 3 to 4 (kanji) per version. A terminator of 3, 5, 7 or 9 zero bits ends the data (shortened at the capacity); then zeros to a codeword boundary and alternating pad codewords 0xEC, 0x11, and a final half codeword of zeros. Anything else (data after the terminator, a wrong pad, a reserved mode indicator, an empty segment, no data) rejects the symbol. Byte data has the `Charset::Undeclared` handling of QR (decided by `breader-content`), kanji is Shift JIS.
8. **Results.** `Symbology::MicroQr`, `SymbolMeta::Qr(QrMeta)` with `version` 1 to 4 for M1 to M4, `mask` 0 to 3, `ec_level` `EcLevel::DetectionOnly` for M1 (a new variant: M1 has no correction level, and `L` would claim something it does not do), `eci` empty. The quad maps module corners (0,0), (d,0), (d,d), (0,d). AIM identifier `]Q1` (see [D-038](../decisions.md#d-038): the ISO/IEC 15424 value was not verified).

## Effort

Fast: same finder scan, at most 4 candidates, no retries (affine-quality mapping from the timing patterns only). Balanced and Exhaustive add, after a grid that passed the structure gates fails to decode: local-threshold sampling from the luma image, and a corner refinement (hill climbing on the four grid corners to maximize the number of unanimous sub-samples plus the agreement of the known patterns) followed by plain and local-threshold sampling again. Exhaustive tries up to 40 candidates per pass.

## False-positive control

A finder and a 15-bit word would be a weak gate, so a symbol must pass in order: finder template at least 62 of 81, both timing lines alternating with the same module count and a quiet zone after them (in the transitions of the image, before any grid is built), the quiet-zone ring, finder and separator, the timing patterns of the grid, the format word within distance 2 of a valid word, the sampled size equal to the size the format names, Reed-Solomon with the guard (M1: no syndrome at all), and a well-formed bitstream with padding. M1, the weakest (16 check bits), has these gates in addition: only numeric data of at most 5 digits, a padding that must be exact. Tests: QR codes of several versions (also with the data area wiped, the other two finders occluded, random damage), checkerboards, stripes, noise and ring-like blobs never read as Micro QR at any effort ([reader tests](../../crates/breader-qr/src/micro/reader_tests.rs)); the regression gates have 0 false positives.

## Evaluation

Gates: `zxing-samples` (`microqrcode-1`, now counted), the synthetic sets (Micro QR images appended after the Aztec ones: all variants but M3-L, every third image mirrored, every class; the shared sets are regenerated at merge time) and every other gate for the false-positive count. Measurements: [BASELINES](../BASELINES.md#micro-qr-reader-2026-10-11). Fuzz target `microqr_grid`.

## As built

- The `qrcode` encoder produces M3-L symbols whose check codewords do not satisfy the Reed-Solomon code as read from the standard: a real M3-L symbol of the zxing-cpp samples (`1.png`) decodes with this decoder, the encoder's do not, and every other level matches in both directions. The tests therefore build M3-L with a small writer in the test code and the synthetic images leave M3-L out.
- A format distance of 3 was measured with the standard's single copy: see BASELINES for how many reads it would have added (it is not enabled).
- The standard's remainder bits do not exist for Micro QR (the module counts 36, 80, 132 and 192 are exactly the codeword bits).

## Known gaps

- Two photographs of the zxing-cpp set (`7.webp`, `9.webp`, 4 to 5 px per module, perspective and blur) are not read: the grid sampling is off by up to a module in the data area, beyond what the check codewords correct. zxing-cpp reads them.
- At Fast, `NoQuietZone.png` and the photograph `MQR-needs-br-update.jpg` are not read (they need the retries).
- Modules of 2 px with a sub-pixel position (anti-aliased) are read only from about 2.5 px; pixel-aligned 2 px symbols read.
- M3-L has only one real sample; the AIM modifier, the encoder discrepancy above and the bit order of the format word are checked against encoders and samples, not against the text of the standard.
